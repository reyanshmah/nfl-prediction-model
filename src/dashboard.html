"Highest Agreement" and "Best Value" answer different questions. Agreement tells you which game both sources are confident about — but a game everyone loves has a terrible payout (a huge favorite might be priced at −900), so being right doesn't mean the bet is profitable. Value is expected value per $1 staked: EV = p × b − (1 − p), where p is the model's own win probability for a side and b is that side's real moneyline payout (e.g. +150 → b=1.5, −175 → b=0.571). A positive EV means the model thinks that side is MORE likely to win than the market's own price implies — an edge, not just a likely outcome. This is a real bet on the model actually beating the market's pricing, which earlier testing in this project found the market usually wins on average — treat this as "where the model disagrees with the market's price," not a guarantee. Note this often surfaces the SAME games as "Highest Disagreement," since a big model/market probability gap is what produces a big EV number — see "Hidden Edge" for a more conservative alternative.
A more conservative kind of "value" than raw EV. Best Value (EV) often rewards games where the model confidently overrides a market that ALSO has a strong opinion — and earlier testing in this project found the market wins more often specifically in those high-disagreement spots (11 of 15 in one head-to-head check). Hidden Edge looks for something different: games where the market has no strong opinion (close to a coin flip) but the model is still confident on one side. That's the model adding information in a genuine toss-up, not claiming to know better than a market that's already made up its mind — arguably the more trustworthy shape of edge, even though it won't always be the highest raw EV number on the board.
Best Value (EV) and Hidden Edge each answer half the "would this bet be good" question on their own: EV checks whether a bet is profitable at the price offered, but its biggest numbers mostly come from the model fighting a market that's already confident — the exact spot this project's own testing found the model loses more often. Hidden Edge avoids that trap but never checks whether the bet is actually profitable at the odds given. Smart Value combines both by multiplying them: score = EV × (1 − marketConfidence) — EV is the reasoning AND the profit measure at once, discounted by market confidence. See "Combined (Model+Market)" for a different way of combining them, where the reasoning and the payout are kept as two separate things instead of one multiplied score.
If you were actually going to bet on one of these games, this is the sort to use. The others above answer a betting-theory question (is this bet mathematically +EV); this one answers a more direct question: given both the model's AND the market's opinions together, how confident should you actually be, and what would you get back? The model and market probabilities are blended into one combined probability — not multiplied with the payout, just used as the reasoning — and the market's real moneyline is shown separately afterward purely as payout info.

The blend weight is dynamic, not fixed: the market's weight never drops below 50% (it's the generally sharper source overall — about 2-3 points more accurate in this project's own testing), rising toward 100% as the market gets more confident. The model only gets real influence specifically when the market has no strong opinion. modelWeight = 0.5 × (1 − marketConfidence). So market 50% / model 80% blends meaningfully toward the model (nothing confident to defer to); market 80% / model 65% stays close to the market's own number (overriding a confident market is the model's known weak spot).
